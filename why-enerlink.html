Why Canadian energy, why now

Canada is one of the world’s most significant energy-producing nations. Historically, limited tidewater access meant most Canadian energy exports were directed to the United States. That landscape is changing — two infrastructure milestones have materially expanded direct, structural access from Western Canada to Asia.

Milestone 01 · Crude oil

Trans Mountain Expansion — May 2024

Nearly tripled system capacity, carrying Alberta crude and refined products to tidewater at Burnaby, BC for shipment to Asia and the U.S. West Coast. Westridge Marine Terminal exports have surged, with heavy crude increasingly destined for Asian refiners.

Milestone 02 · LNG

LNG Canada — first cargo June 2025

Canada’s first large-scale LNG export facility began loading cargoes from Kitimat, BC, creating a major Pacific export route for Western Canadian natural gas — shorter sailing times to Asia than the U.S. Gulf Coast, with no Panama Canal transit.

The case for diversification

Energy Security Begins with Diversification

Asian economies rely heavily on a limited set of supply regions and a small number of maritime chokepoints. Recent geopolitical and supply-chain disruptions have shown the risk of depending too heavily on any single supplier, region or route.

Diversification is not simply a search for the cheapest barrel — it is a matter of national energy security. Canada’s Pacific gateway offers a politically stable, rule-of-law supply relationship on established trans-Pacific shipping lanes, away from congested chokepoints.

EnerLink supports this shift by connecting qualified Asian buyers with credible North American supply sources and project opportunities.
